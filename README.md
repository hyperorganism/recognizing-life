# Recognizing Life

What observations distinguish living organization from plausible nonliving alternatives? Definitions, environmental context, measurement limits and false positives belong in the inference.

## Definitions and organizational criteria

- **Negative entropy and aperiodic crystals** - Schrödinger argues that organisms avoid decay to equilibrium by drawing free energy from their surroundings and exporting entropy. He proposes that genes are a stable "aperiodic crystal" whose structure encodes a hereditary code-script. [Schrödinger 1944](<https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139644129> "What is Life?: With Mind and Matter and Autobiographical Sketches; Cambridge University Press, 1992 Canto reissue").
    - **Dissipative structures** - Open systems driven far from equilibrium can sustain order in convection cells and chemical oscillations. These nonliving examples show why entropy export alone cannot identify life. [Prigogine 1978](<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.201.4358.777> "Time, Structure, and Fluctuations; Science").
- **Defining life** - Argues that definitions built from familiar organisms face persistent counterexamples. A general theory of living systems, rather than a longer checklist, is needed to interpret unfamiliar cases. [Cleland and Chyba 2002](<https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1020503324273> "Defining &#x27;life&#x27;; Orig. Life Evol. Biosph.").

## Biosignatures and competing explanations

- **Ladder of Life Detection** - Relates candidate biosignatures to measurement quality, context and abiotic alternatives. Developed for robotic searches for microbial life; its decision rules are a revisable framework, not a universal detector. [Neveu et al. 2018](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6211372/> "The Ladder of Life Detection; Astrobiology").
- **Molecular assembly as a biosignature** - Estimates molecular assembly complexity from mass-spectral fragmentation. High values distinguish biological samples in the tested collection; the proposed indicator concerns molecules and their production histories. [Marshall et al. 2021](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-23258-x> "Identifying molecules as biosignatures with assembly theory and mass spectrometry; Nat. Commun.").
    - **Assembly and selection** - Combines the minimum recursive construction steps for an object with its abundance to quantify selection in an assembly space. The framework links repeated complex objects to production histories; assembly index alone is insufficient. [Sharma et al. 2023](<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06600-9> "Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution; Nature").
    - **Assembly thresholds across materials** - Hazen et al. calculate high indices for abiotic mineral substructures. Walker et al. dispute transferring thresholds between those calculations and measured organic molecules, emphasizing building blocks, abundance and measurement. [Hazen et al. 2024](<https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2023.0632> "Molecular assembly indices of mineral heteropolyanions: some abiotic molecules are as complex as large biomolecules; J. R. Soc. Interface"), [Walker et al. 2024](<https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2024.0367> "Experimentally measured assembly indices are required to determine the threshold for life; J. R. Soc. Interface").

## Open questions

- Which proposed indicators discriminate unfamiliar living systems from abiotic controls outside the datasets used to develop them?
- How do system boundaries, spatial scale, observation duration and environmental context change which organization we can detect?
